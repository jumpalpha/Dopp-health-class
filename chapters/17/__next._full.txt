2e:T17d4,<section id="part-0" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 1 — Conception &amp; Early Development</h3>
              <p>Human life begins at fertilization — when a sperm cell penetrates an egg to form a single-celled zygote with a unique combination of 46 chromosomes.</p>
              <div class="reading-grid">
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Zygote (0–2 wks)</strong><span>Fertilized egg; travels to uterus and implants in the endometrium</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Embryo (2–8 wks)</strong><span>Rapid cell division; major organ systems begin forming</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Fetus (8 wks–birth)</strong><span>All major structures present; continues growing and maturing</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Placenta</strong><span>Organ connecting mother and fetus; transfers nutrients and oxygen; removes waste</span></div>
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> What is the difference between a zygote, embryo, and fetus?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-1" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 2 — Prenatal Care &amp; Development</h3>
              <p>Prenatal care — medical care during pregnancy — significantly improves outcomes for mother and baby. The developing fetus is vulnerable to environmental factors called teratogens.</p>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <strong>Critical Periods</strong><br>
                The first trimester (weeks 1–12) is the most critical for organ development. Exposure to alcohol, tobacco, drugs, or certain medications during this time can cause birth defects.
              </div>
              <ul class="reader-list">
                <li>Folic acid before/during pregnancy reduces neural tube defects</li>
                <li>No alcohol — Fetal Alcohol Spectrum Disorder (FASD) is 100% preventable</li>
                <li>Avoid tobacco — linked to low birth weight and premature birth</li>
                <li>Regular OB/GYN visits track fetal growth and maternal health</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> What is a teratogen, and why is the first trimester most vulnerable?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-2" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 3 — Labor, Birth &amp; the Newborn</h3>
              <p>A typical pregnancy lasts ~40 weeks (280 days). Labor involves three stages: cervical dilation, delivery of the baby, and delivery of the placenta.</p>
              <div class="reading-grid">
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Stage 1</strong><span>Cervix dilates to 10 cm; contractions become stronger and closer together</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Stage 2</strong><span>Baby moves through birth canal and is delivered</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Stage 3</strong><span>Placenta (afterbirth) is delivered</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Cesarean Section</strong><span>Surgical delivery through the abdomen when vaginal birth is not safe</span></div>
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> What happens during each of the three stages of labor?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-3" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 4 — Infancy &amp; Early Childhood</h3>
              <p>The first years of life involve the most rapid growth and brain development a human ever experiences. Early experiences literally shape brain architecture.</p>
              <ul class="reader-checklist">
                <li>Breastfeeding provides optimal nutrition and immune protection for infants</li>
                <li>Secure attachment to caregivers is essential for emotional development</li>
                <li>Sensory stimulation (talking, reading, play) builds neural connections</li>
                <li>Vaccinations protect infants from serious preventable diseases</li>
                <li>Safe sleep practices prevent Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS)</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> Why are the first few years of life so critical for brain development?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-4" class="reader-section vocab-section"><h3>Chapter Vocabulary</h3>
              <div class="vocab-table">
                <div><strong>Fertilization</strong><span>The union of a sperm and egg cell to form a zygote.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Zygote</strong><span>The single cell formed when a sperm fertilizes an egg; contains 46 chromosomes.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Embryo</strong><span>The developing human from fertilization through 8 weeks, when major organs begin forming.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Fetus</strong><span>The developing human from 8 weeks after fertilization until birth.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Placenta</strong><span>The organ that connects mother and fetus to transfer nutrients, oxygen, and remove waste.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Teratogen</strong><span>Any substance or condition that can interfere with fetal development and cause birth defects.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Prenatal care</strong><span>Medical care received during pregnancy to monitor the health of mother and developing baby.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Trimester</strong><span>One of three ~13-week periods that divide a pregnancy.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Labor</strong><span>The process by which the uterus contracts to deliver the baby and placenta.</span></div>
                <div><strong>SIDS</strong><span>Sudden Infant Death Syndrome — the unexplained death of an otherwise healthy infant during sleep.</span></div>
