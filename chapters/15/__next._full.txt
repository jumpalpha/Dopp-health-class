2c:T19bb,<section id="part-0" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 1 — The Cardiovascular System</h3>
              <p>The cardiovascular system — heart, blood, and blood vessels — circulates oxygen and nutrients to every cell while removing carbon dioxide and waste products.</p>
              <div class="reading-grid">
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Heart</strong><span>4-chambered pump; right side pumps blood to lungs; left side pumps to body</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Arteries</strong><span>Carry oxygenated blood away from heart; thick muscular walls</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Veins</strong><span>Return deoxygenated blood to heart; contain valves to prevent backflow</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Capillaries</strong><span>Tiny vessels where gas and nutrient exchange occurs between blood and cells</span></div>
              </div>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <strong>Heart Rate Facts</strong><br>
                Resting heart rate for teens: 60–100 bpm. Athletes can have resting HR as low as 40 bpm — a sign of a strong, efficient heart. Regular aerobic exercise lowers resting heart rate over time.
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> What is the difference between arteries and veins?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-1" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 2 — The Respiratory System</h3>
              <p>The respiratory system brings oxygen into the body and expels carbon dioxide. Every breath moves air through a series of structures that filter, warm, and deliver it to the lungs.</p>
              <ul class="reader-list">
                <li><strong>Nose/mouth</strong> → <strong>pharynx</strong> → <strong>larynx</strong> → <strong>trachea</strong> → <strong>bronchi</strong> → <strong>bronchioles</strong> → <strong>alveoli</strong></li>
                <li>Alveoli are tiny air sacs where gas exchange occurs — O₂ in, CO₂ out</li>
                <li>Diaphragm — dome-shaped muscle that powers breathing</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <strong>Smoking &amp; Lung Damage</strong><br>
                Tobacco smoke destroys cilia (tiny hairs that trap debris), damages alveoli, and causes chronic inflammation. This leads to COPD, emphysema, and lung cancer — often irreversible.
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> Where does gas exchange occur in the lungs?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-2" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 3 — The Digestive System</h3>
              <p>The digestive system breaks food into nutrients the body can absorb. This involves mechanical (physical) and chemical (enzyme-based) digestion across ~30 feet of digestive tract.</p>
              <div class="reading-grid">
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Mouth</strong><span>Mechanical digestion begins; salivary amylase starts breaking down starch</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Stomach</strong><span>Churns food; HCl kills bacteria; pepsin digests proteins</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Small Intestine</strong><span>Most nutrient absorption; 20 ft long; villi increase surface area</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Large Intestine</strong><span>Water absorption; houses gut microbiome; forms waste</span></div>
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> What is the difference between mechanical and chemical digestion?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-3" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 4 — Keeping These Systems Healthy</h3>
              <p>Lifestyle choices profoundly affect cardiovascular, respiratory, and digestive health. Small daily habits compound into major differences over a lifetime.</p>
              <ul class="reader-checklist">
                <li>Aerobic exercise strengthens the heart and improves circulation</li>
                <li>Never smoke — tobacco is the leading cause of preventable cardiovascular and lung disease</li>
                <li>Eat fiber-rich foods to support healthy digestion and gut microbiome</li>
                <li>Stay hydrated — water is essential for blood volume and digestion</li>
                <li>Limit saturated fat and sodium — both elevate cardiovascular disease risk</li>
                <li>Know warning signs: chest pain, shortness of breath, persistent heartburn</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> Name two lifestyle choices that protect cardiovascular health.</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-4" class="reader-section vocab-section"><h3>Chapter Vocabulary</h3>
              <div class="vocab-table">
                <div><strong>Cardiovascular system</strong><span>The organ system of the heart and blood vessels that circulates blood throughout the body.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Artery</strong><span>A blood vessel that carries oxygenated blood away from the heart.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Vein</strong><span>A blood vessel that returns deoxygenated blood back to the heart.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Capillary</strong><span>The smallest blood vessels where gas and nutrient exchange occurs between blood and cells.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Alveoli</strong><span>Tiny air sacs in the lungs where oxygen and carbon dioxide are exchanged.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Diaphragm</strong><span>The dome-shaped muscle below the lungs that controls breathing.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Peristalsis</strong><span>The wave-like muscle contractions that move food through the digestive tract.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Villi</strong><span>Tiny finger-like projections in the small intestine that absorb nutrients.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Cholesterol</strong><span>A fatty substance in the blood; high levels increase risk of heart disease.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Hypertension</strong><span>Persistently high blood pressure; a major risk factor for heart attack and stroke.</span></div>
              </div>
