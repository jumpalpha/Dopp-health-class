24:T1bb0,<section id="part-0" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 1 — Safe and Healthy Friendships</h3>
  <h4>Types of Friendships</h4>
  <p>Your <em>peers</em>, people your age with similar interests, have a powerful influence on your health. Strong friendships share similar values, honest communication, mutual respect, concern for each other's safety, and sharing of joys and disappointments.</p>
  <div class="reading-grid compact">
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Casual</h3><p>Built on shared interests without deep emotional bonds. You enjoy each other but don't always confide.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Close</h3><p>Deep emotional ties. You can share thoughts, feelings and struggles without judgment.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Platonic</h3><p>A friendship with someone of a different gender, with real affection but no romance.</p></div>
  </div>
  <h4>Online Friendships — Stay Safe</h4>
  <ul class="reader-checklist">
    <li>Never share your full name, address, phone number or school.</li>
    <li>Don't send photos to people you haven't met in person.</li>
    <li>Never meet an online contact alone. Always tell a trusted adult first.</li>
    <li>If someone online makes you uncomfortable, tell an adult right away.</li>
    <li>Be careful in gaming chats, social media DMs and apps that connect you with strangers.</li>
  </ul>
  <div class="reading-callout"><div class="callout-label">Key Idea</div><div>A <em>clique</em> can feel like belonging, but it often comes with pressure to conform and to leave others out. Healthy friendships expand your world. They don't shrink it.</div></div>
</section>
<section id="part-1" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 2 — Peer Pressure and Refusal Skills</h3>
  <h4>What Is Peer Pressure?</h4>
  <p><em>Peer pressure</em> is the influence people your age have on your actions and decisions. It can be direct or subtle. Not all of it is bad: positive peer pressure can push you to try new activities, volunteer, work harder and avoid risky behavior.</p>
  <p>Negative pressure can show up as <em>harassment</em> (repeatedly targeting someone) or <em>manipulation</em> (indirect, dishonest control through guilt trips, threats, flattery or bribes).</p>
  <h4>Three Refusal Steps</h4>
  <ul class="reader-list">
    <li><strong>1. State your position.</strong> Say no clearly and firmly, with an honest reason: "It goes against my values." Use confident body language.</li>
    <li><strong>2. Suggest alternatives.</strong> "No, let's go to the movies instead." You stay connected without giving up your values.</li>
    <li><strong>3. Stand your ground.</strong> If the pressure keeps coming, make it clear you won't budge. Walking away is always an option.</li>
  </ul>
  <div class="reading-callout"><div class="callout-label">Try This</div><div>Practice your refusals out loud before you're in a high-pressure moment. It's much easier to hold your ground when you already know what you'll say.</div></div>
</section>
<section id="part-2" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 3 — Practicing Abstinence</h3>
  <h4>Dating and Setting Limits</h4>
  <p>Dating can be a positive experience when both people respect each other. The most important limit you can set is <em>abstinence</em>: a deliberate decision to avoid high-risk behaviors, including sexual activity and the use of alcohol, tobacco and other drugs. <em>Self-control</em> means letting your values, not the emotions of the moment, guide your choices.</p>
  <h4>Why Abstinence Matters</h4>
  <div class="reading-grid compact">
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Unplanned pregnancy</h3><p>Teen birth rates are at a record low, but an unplanned pregnancy still changes the course of thousands of young lives every year.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>STIs</h3><p>People ages 15–24 are about 13% of the population but have nearly half of new STIs. Abstinence is the only 100% effective protection.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Emotional risks</h3><p>Hurt, guilt, loss of self-respect and regret are common when teens aren't ready for a sexual relationship.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Media pressure</h3><p>Shows and social media glamorize sex without showing consequences. That's entertainment, not reality.</p></div>
  </div>
  <h4>Handling Pressure Lines</h4>
  <ul class="reader-list">
    <li>"Everybody does it." → "No. Not everybody is doing it."</li>
    <li>"No one will know." → "I'll know, and I'm the one who matters."</li>
    <li>"If you loved me, you'd do it." → "If you loved me, you'd respect my decision."</li>
  </ul>
  <div class="reading-callout"><div class="callout-label">Key Idea</div><div>It's never too late. Anyone can choose abstinence going forward, and it's a positive choice that protects your health and future.</div></div>
</section>
