2d:T18cd,<section id="part-0" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 1 — What's in Tobacco?</h3>
              <p>Tobacco contains over 7,000 chemicals when burned, including hundreds that are toxic and at least 70 known carcinogens. Understanding what you're inhaling is the foundation of informed refusal.</p>
              <div class="reading-grid">
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Nicotine</strong><span>The addictive chemical in tobacco; stimulates the brain's reward centers</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Carbon Monoxide</strong><span>Bonds to hemoglobin better than oxygen; reduces oxygen delivery to tissues</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Tar</strong><span>Sticky residue that coats lungs; contains most carcinogens</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Formaldehyde</strong><span>Used in embalming; damages lung tissue with every cigarette</span></div>
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> How does carbon monoxide harm the body?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-1" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 2 — Health Consequences of Tobacco</h3>
              <p>Tobacco is the leading cause of preventable death in the United States — responsible for 480,000 deaths per year. It damages virtually every organ system.</p>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <strong>Diseases Caused by Smoking</strong><br>
                Lung cancer, COPD, emphysema, chronic bronchitis, oral cancer, esophageal cancer, heart disease, stroke, peripheral artery disease, and reduced fertility — all linked to tobacco use.
              </div>
              <ul class="reader-list">
                <li>Smokers die on average 10 years younger than non-smokers</li>
                <li>Secondhand smoke causes 41,000 deaths/year among non-smokers (CDC)</li>
                <li>Teen smokers lose lung function faster than non-smoking peers</li>
                <li>Nicotine is especially harmful to the developing adolescent brain</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> Name three diseases caused by tobacco use.</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-2" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 3 — Vaping &amp; E-Cigarettes</h3>
              <p>E-cigarettes (vapes) heat liquid containing nicotine, propylene glycol, vegetable glycerin, and flavorings to create an aerosol. They were marketed as "safer" — but evidence tells a different story.</p>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <strong>EVALI</strong><br>
                In 2019, thousands of people were hospitalized with EVALI (e-cigarette or vaping product use-associated lung injury), many of them teens. The outbreak was linked to Vitamin E acetate in some products.
              </div>
              <ul class="reader-checklist">
                <li>Vaping delivers highly concentrated nicotine — one JUUL pod = ~20 cigarettes of nicotine</li>
                <li>Adolescent vapers are 3x more likely to start smoking cigarettes</li>
                <li>Flavors (mango, mint, candy) are specifically designed to attract teens</li>
                <li>Long-term effects of vaping are still being studied — the risk is real</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> Why are flavored e-cigarette products especially concerning for teens?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-3" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 4 — Quitting &amp; Prevention</h3>
              <p>Nicotine addiction is powerful — but quitting is possible. Most smokers try multiple times before successfully quitting, and that's normal. Each attempt teaches something valuable.</p>
              <ul class="reader-list">
                <li>FDA-approved cessation aids: nicotine replacement (patch, gum, lozenge), varenicline (Chantix), bupropion</li>
                <li>Behavioral counseling doubles quit success rates</li>
                <li>Set a quit date, tell someone, remove tobacco from your environment</li>
                <li>Identify triggers (stress, social situations) and plan alternatives</li>
                <li>smokefree.gov offers free tools and apps for quitting</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> What are two strategies that increase the success of quitting tobacco?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-4" class="reader-section vocab-section"><h3>Chapter Vocabulary</h3>
              <div class="vocab-table">
                <div><strong>Nicotine</strong><span>The highly addictive chemical found in tobacco that stimulates the brain's reward pathway.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Tar</strong><span>The sticky brown residue from tobacco smoke that coats the lungs and contains carcinogens.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Carbon monoxide</strong><span>A colorless, odorless gas in tobacco smoke that reduces oxygen delivery in the blood.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Carcinogen</strong><span>Any substance capable of causing cancer in living tissue.</span></div>
                <div><strong>COPD</strong><span>Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease — a group of lung diseases including emphysema and chronic bronchitis.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Emphysema</strong><span>A lung disease where alveoli are destroyed, causing severe breathing difficulty.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Secondhand smoke</strong><span>Tobacco smoke inhaled by a non-smoker in the vicinity of someone smoking.</span></div>
                <div><strong>E-cigarette</strong><span>An electronic device that heats a liquid to create an aerosol containing nicotine and other chemicals.</span></div>
                <div><strong>EVALI</strong><span>E-cigarette or Vaping Product Use-Associated Lung Injury — a serious lung condition linked to vaping.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Cessation</strong><span>The act of stopping a behavior, such as quitting tobacco use.</span></div>
