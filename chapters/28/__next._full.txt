2d:T1b40,<section id="part-0" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 1 — Community Health</h3>
              <p>Community health refers to the physical, mental, and social well-being of a defined group of people. It goes beyond individual health — the zip code you grow up in predicts health outcomes as much as your genetic code.</p>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <strong>Social Determinants of Health</strong><br>
                The non-medical factors that influence health: income, education, housing, employment, food access, transportation, and social support. Addressing these "upstream" factors prevents disease more effectively than treatment alone.
              </div>
              <div class="reading-grid">
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Public Health</strong><span>The science of protecting and improving the health of communities through education, policy, and research</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Health Disparities</strong><span>Preventable differences in disease burden between groups due to social, economic, and environmental factors</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Community Resources</strong><span>Health departments, clinics, food banks, shelters — local systems that support community health</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Advocacy</strong><span>Working to change policies, systems, and environments that affect community health</span></div>
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> What are social determinants of health, and why do they matter?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-1" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 2 — Air &amp; Water Quality</h3>
              <p>Clean air and safe water are fundamental public health needs. Pollution affects respiratory health, cardiovascular health, and cognitive development — especially in children.</p>
              <ul class="reader-list">
                <li>Indoor air pollutants: radon, carbon monoxide, mold, tobacco smoke, VOCs</li>
                <li>Outdoor air: smog (ozone + particulate matter) worsens asthma and heart disease</li>
                <li>Clean Water Act (1972) regulates discharge of pollutants into U.S. waterways</li>
                <li>Lead in water (e.g., Flint, MI) causes permanent neurological damage — especially in children</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> Name two indoor and two outdoor air pollutants that affect health.</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-2" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 3 — Climate Change &amp; Health</h3>
              <p>Climate change is recognized by every major medical organization as the greatest health threat of the 21st century. Its effects are already measurable and accelerating.</p>
              <div class="reading-grid">
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Heat</strong><span>Record heat waves cause heat stroke and cardiovascular stress; urban areas hit hardest</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Extreme Weather</strong><span>Stronger hurricanes, floods, wildfires displace communities and contaminate water supplies</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Vector Expansion</strong><span>Warmer temperatures expand mosquito/tick ranges — spreading Lyme, Zika, dengue</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Mental Health</strong><span>Eco-anxiety and climate grief are real; disaster trauma causes PTSD</span></div>
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> Name two ways climate change directly affects human health.</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-3" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 4 — Being a Health Advocate</h3>
              <p>You don't need to be a politician to make a difference. Health advocacy starts locally — in your school, neighborhood, and community — and builds from there.</p>
              <ul class="reader-checklist">
                <li>Vote and engage with local government — zoning, parks, food access, and transit decisions affect health</li>
                <li>Reduce personal carbon footprint: plant-based meals, public transit, energy efficiency</li>
                <li>Support community health organizations and volunteer</li>
                <li>Use your voice — write letters, attend school board meetings, start or join clubs</li>
                <li>Share accurate health information — counter misinformation in your networks</li>
                <li>Champion health equity — advocate for under-resourced communities</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> Name two local actions a teenager can take to improve community health.</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-4" class="reader-section vocab-section"><h3>Chapter Vocabulary</h3>
              <div class="vocab-table">
                <div><strong>Community health</strong><span>The physical, mental, and social well-being of a defined group of people living in a shared environment.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Public health</strong><span>The science of protecting and improving the health of populations through education, policy, and preventive measures.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Social determinants of health</strong><span>Non-medical factors (income, education, housing) that significantly influence health outcomes.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Health disparity</strong><span>A preventable difference in health outcomes between populations linked to social, economic, or environmental disadvantage.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Environmental health</strong><span>The field studying how the natural and built environment affects human health.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Air quality index</strong><span>A measure of how clean or polluted outdoor air is and what health effects may be of concern.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Greenhouse gases</strong><span>Gases (CO₂, methane) that trap heat in the atmosphere, contributing to climate change.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Health advocacy</strong><span>Actions taken to promote policies, systems, and conditions that improve health for individuals and communities.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Health equity</strong><span>The state in which every person has a fair opportunity to be as healthy as possible.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Sustainability</strong><span>Meeting present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.</span></div>
