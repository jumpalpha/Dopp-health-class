25:T2b9d,<section id="part-0" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 1 — Developing Your Self-Esteem</h3>
              <h4>What Is Mental and Emotional Health?</h4>
              <p><strong>Mental and emotional health</strong> is your ability to accept yourself and others, express and manage your emotions, and handle the demands and challenges life throws at you. When you're struggling emotionally it often shows up as poor sleep, unhealthy eating, or strained relationships.</p>

              <h4>Signs of Good Mental and Emotional Health</h4>
              <ul class="reader-list">
                <li><strong>Sense of belonging:</strong> Feeling connected to family, friends, and your community</li>
                <li><strong>Sense of purpose:</strong> Believing your life has meaning and direction</li>
                <li><strong>Positive outlook:</strong> Seeing the bright side even when things are hard</li>
                <li><strong>Self-sufficiency:</strong> Trusting yourself to make responsible decisions</li>
                <li><strong>Healthy self-esteem:</strong> Believing in your own worth, even when you make mistakes</li>
              </ul>

              <p>People with good mental and emotional health are <strong>resilient</strong> — they have the ability to adapt and bounce back from disappointment, difficulty, or crisis. Everyone faces hard times; resilience is what helps you get through them.</p>

              <h4>Building Self-Esteem</h4>
              <p><strong>Self-esteem</strong> is how much you value, respect, and feel confident about yourself. It shapes your outlook and influences the health choices you make. It grows when you push through challenges and use <strong>positive self-talk</strong> — the encouraging inner voice that reminds you of your worth.</p>
              <ul class="reader-checklist">
                <li>Choose friends who genuinely value and respect you</li>
                <li>Replace negative self-talk with supportive self-talk</li>
                <li>See mistakes as learning opportunities, not failures</li>
                <li>Try new activities to discover your strengths</li>
                <li>Volunteer — helping others is one of the fastest ways to feel good about yourself</li>
                <li>Focus on progress, not perfection</li>
              </ul>

              <h4>Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs</h4>
              <p>Psychologist Abraham Maslow created a model explaining what motivates human growth. The <strong>hierarchy of needs</strong> is a ranked list of needs essential to development, starting at the most basic and building upward:</p>
              <ul class="reader-list">
                <li><strong>Physical needs:</strong> Food, water, sleep, shelter</li>
                <li><strong>Safety needs:</strong> Being secure from danger</li>
                <li><strong>Belonging needs:</strong> Love, connection, feeling accepted</li>
                <li><strong>Esteem needs:</strong> Feeling valued and recognized</li>
                <li><strong>Self-actualization:</strong> Reaching your full potential</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <div class="callout-label">Key Idea</div>
                <div>You need to meet lower-level needs before you can focus on higher ones — and it matters <em>how</em> you meet those needs. Joining a gang to feel accepted, for example, is a high-risk behavior that damages all sides of your health.</div>
              </div>
            </section>

            <section id="part-1" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 2 — Developing Personal Identity and Character</h3>
              <h4>Your Personal Identity</h4>
              <p>Your <strong>personal identity</strong> is your sense of yourself as a unique individual — your values, interests, beliefs, strengths, and relationships. It's shaped by your family, culture, friendships, and experiences, and continues developing throughout your teen years and beyond.</p>

              <h4>The 6 Traits of Good Character</h4>
              <ul class="reader-list">
                <li><strong>Trustworthiness:</strong> Honest, reliable, and loyal. You do what you say you'll do.</li>
                <li><strong>Respect:</strong> Treat people and their property with care, even when you disagree.</li>
                <li><strong>Responsibility:</strong> Think before you act, own your choices, and follow through.</li>
                <li><strong>Fairness:</strong> Play by the rules, share, and don't take advantage of others.</li>
                <li><strong>Caring:</strong> Show kindness, gratitude, and compassion toward others.</li>
                <li><strong>Citizenship:</strong> Contribute to your school and community, follow laws, and advocate for a healthy environment.</li>
              </ul>

              <p>A person of good character demonstrates these values consistently through <strong>integrity</strong> — living by core ethical values even when no one is watching.</p>

              <h4>Building a Healthy Identity</h4>
              <ul class="reader-checklist">
                <li>Know your strengths and weaknesses — honestly and without being too hard on yourself</li>
                <li>Surround yourself with positive, supportive people</li>
                <li>Develop a sense of purpose through short-term and long-term goals</li>
                <li>Build meaningful relationships — people who give you constructive criticism are your most valuable supporters</li>
                <li>Give back to your community through volunteering</li>
              </ul>
            </section>

            <section id="part-2" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 3 — Expressing Emotions in Healthful Ways</h3>
              <h4>Understanding Your Emotions</h4>
              <p><strong>Emotions</strong> are signals that tell your mind and body how to react. They're neither good nor bad — what matters is how you choose to express them. During the teen years, <strong>hormones</strong> can make emotions feel more intense and unpredictable.</p>
              <div class="reading-check">
                <strong>Reality Check</strong>
                <span>According to the CDC, about <strong>40% of high school students</strong> reported persistent sadness or hopelessness in 2023.</span>
              </div>

              <h4>Common Emotions</h4>
              <ul class="reader-list">
                <li><strong>Happiness:</strong> Feeling satisfied and positive; brings energy and creativity</li>
                <li><strong>Sadness:</strong> A normal reaction to loss or disappointment — it's okay to feel sad</li>
                <li><strong>Love:</strong> Deep affection, care, and respect for someone</li>
                <li><strong>Fear:</strong> A natural response to danger; can become a problem if it stops healthy risk-taking</li>
                <li><strong>Guilt:</strong> Regret when you act against your values — sometimes felt over things beyond your control</li>
                <li><strong>Anger:</strong> Normal reaction to hurt or harm — becomes <strong>hostility</strong> when intentionally harmful toward others</li>
              </ul>

              <h4>Managing Your Emotions</h4>
              <p>When emotions feel overwhelming: take slow deep breaths, relax your muscles, remove yourself from the situation, write in a private journal, or talk to someone you trust.</p>
              <p>Some people use <strong>defense mechanisms</strong> — mental processes that protect from intense emotional pain. Common ones include: Repression, Denial, Projection, Rationalization, Suppression, Regression, and Compensation. These can provide short-term relief, but relying on them too long prevents you from solving the actual problem.</p>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <div class="callout-label">Anger Tip</div>
                <div>When you feel anger building, <strong>PAUSE</strong>. Try deep breathing, remove yourself from the situation, channel the energy into something physical, or talk it out. <strong>Empathy</strong> — understanding how the other person feels — can also help you cool down and see the full picture.</div>
              </div>
            </section>
