1f:T23c4,<section id="part-0" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 1 — Causes of Conflict</h3>
  <p>A <em>conflict</em> is any disagreement, struggle or fight. Because people are different, conflicts are a normal part of life. The goal is to manage them before they get out of hand. Handled well, conflicts can actually make relationships stronger.</p>
  <h4>Common Causes</h4>
  <div class="reading-grid compact">
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Misunderstandings</h3><p>Two people read the same thing differently, especially across backgrounds, cultures or ages.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Power struggles</h3><p>Who gets to make the decisions or set the rules.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Jealousy &amp; envy</h3><p>Feeling left behind or replaced.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Property disputes</h3><p>Arguments over borrowed or damaged belongings.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Conflicting values</h3><p>Different beliefs about how people should act.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Lack of respect</h3><p>Rudeness, prejudice or treating someone as less than equal.</p></div>
  </div>
  <div class="reading-callout"><div class="callout-label">Key Idea</div><div>The feelings most likely to make a conflict <em>escalate</em> (get more serious) are hurt pride, embarrassment and the desire for revenge. Noticing them in yourself is one of the best ways to prevent violence.</div></div>
</section>
<section id="part-1" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 2 — Resolving Conflicts</h3>
  <h4>Negotiation, Step by Step</h4>
  <ul class="reader-list">
    <li><strong>1. Calm down first.</strong> Never negotiate while emotions are too hot to think clearly.</li>
    <li><strong>2. Each side explains.</strong> Use "I" messages ("I feel… when… because…") and active listening. No interrupting.</li>
    <li><strong>3. Ask clarifying questions</strong> so both sides really understand each other.</li>
    <li><strong>4. Brainstorm solutions</strong> without judging them yet.</li>
    <li><strong>5. Weigh the pros and cons</strong> together.</li>
    <li><strong>6. Agree on a solution.</strong> Aim for win-win, or the best compromise.</li>
    <li><strong>7. Follow up</strong> later to see if it worked.</li>
  </ul>
  <h4>Mediation and Peer Mediation</h4>
  <p>When negotiating isn't enough, <em>mediation</em> brings in a neutral third party. In <em>peer mediation</em>, trained students help classmates settle conflicts. The keys are neutrality, <em>confidentiality</em> and clear ground rules.</p>
  <div class="reading-callout"><div class="callout-label">Important</div><div>Peer mediation is NOT for situations involving violence, weapons or crimes. Those go to school administrators or law enforcement.</div></div>
</section>
<section id="part-2" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 3 — Understanding Violence</h3>
  <p><em>Violence</em> is the threatened or actual use of physical force to harm a person or damage property. Uncontrolled anger, the need to control others, prejudice and revenge all contribute. Alcohol and drugs make violence more likely because they lower inhibitions and impair judgment.</p>
  <div class="reading-callout"><div class="callout-label">Firearm Safety</div><div>Guns are now the leading cause of death for U.S. children and teens. Teens who carry guns are more likely to be hurt by one. Don't carry a weapon. Guns at home should be stored unloaded and locked.</div></div>
  <h4>Types of Violence</h4>
  <div class="reading-grid compact">
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Assault</h3><p>An unlawful physical attack or threat of attack. About half happen between people who know each other.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>School threats</h3><p>Know your school's emergency plan. Report threats, even ones that sound like jokes. Speaking up saves lives.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Sexual violence</h3><p>Any unwelcome sexual contact. Most victims are under 18.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Hate crimes</h3><p>Crimes motivated by hatred of a group because of race, religion, disability, sexual orientation or other traits.</p></div>
  </div>
  <h4>Bullying and Cyberbullying</h4>
  <p>Bullying is repeated, deliberate hurtful behavior where one side has more power. Cyberbullying happens through texts, social media and games, and it can follow you home. Don't respond or retaliate. Save the evidence, block the person, and tell a trusted adult. When you see it happen, be an <strong>upstander</strong>: speak up, support the target, or get help.</p>
</section>
<section id="part-3" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 4 — Preventing and Overcoming Abuse</h3>
  <h4>Warning Signs of an Abusive Relationship</h4>
  <ul class="reader-checklist">
    <li>Pressures you into sexual activity</li>
    <li>Pushes the relationship to be serious or exclusive very fast</li>
    <li>Acts jealous or possessive, controlling where you go or who you see</li>
    <li>Yells, swears or attacks you emotionally</li>
    <li>Threatens physical violence</li>
  </ul>
  <div class="reading-callout"><div class="callout-label">Key Idea</div><div>Controlling behavior is NOT love. These are warning signs of abuse, not signs of caring.</div></div>
  <h4>Forms of Abuse</h4>
  <div class="reading-grid compact">
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Physical</h3><p>Hitting, kicking, shoving, biting or throwing things.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Emotional</h3><p>Verbal abuse, humiliation, threats or isolating someone from friends and family.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Sexual</h3><p>Any unwanted sexual contact or pressure.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Stalking</h3><p>Repeatedly following, harassing or threatening someone, in person or online.</p></div>
  </div>
  <p>About 1 in 9 high school girls reports sexual violence by a dating partner. Alcohol is a major factor, and some attackers use date rape drugs. Never leave your drink unattended, and don't finish a drink that smells or tastes strange.</p>
  <div class="reading-callout"><div class="callout-label">Get Help</div><div>RAINN Sexual Assault Hotline: <strong>1-800-656-4673</strong> · love is respect (teen dating abuse): text LOVEIS to 22522 · 988 Suicide &amp; Crisis Lifeline. Survivors deserve support, not shame. Abuse is never the victim's fault.</div></div>
</section>
