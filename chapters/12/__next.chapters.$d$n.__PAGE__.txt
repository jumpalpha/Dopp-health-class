25:T1b3c,<section id="part-0" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 1 — Benefits of Physical Activity</h3>
              <p>Physical activity is any body movement that uses muscles and burns energy. Regular activity strengthens the heart, builds strong bones, improves mental health, and reduces chronic disease risk.</p>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <strong>CDC Recommendation</strong><br>
                Teens should get at least <strong>60 minutes</strong> of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity every day, including aerobic, muscle-strengthening, and bone-strengthening activities.
              </div>
              <div class="reading-grid">
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Physical Benefits</strong><span>Stronger heart, lungs, muscles, bones; healthy weight; better sleep</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Mental Benefits</strong><span>Reduced anxiety/depression; better focus; improved self-esteem</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Social Benefits</strong><span>Teamwork, cooperation, friendship, belonging</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Academic Benefits</strong><span>Better concentration, memory, and classroom performance</span></div>
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> Name one physical and one mental benefit of regular physical activity.</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-1" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 2 — Types of Fitness</h3>
              <p>Physical fitness has five health-related components and six skill-related components. Building all areas creates well-rounded fitness.</p>
              <ul class="reader-list">
                <li><strong>Cardiorespiratory endurance</strong> — heart/lung efficiency during sustained activity</li>
                <li><strong>Muscular strength</strong> — maximum force a muscle can exert</li>
                <li><strong>Muscular endurance</strong> — ability to repeat muscle contractions over time</li>
                <li><strong>Flexibility</strong> — range of motion at joints</li>
                <li><strong>Body composition</strong> — ratio of fat to lean tissue</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <strong>Skill-Related Fitness</strong><br>
                Agility, balance, coordination, power, reaction time, and speed are skill-related components important for sports and daily movement.
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> What is the difference between muscular strength and muscular endurance?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-2" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 3 — The FITT Principle</h3>
              <p>The <strong>FITT principle</strong> guides how to design an effective fitness program. Each letter stands for a key variable:</p>
              <div class="reading-grid">
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>F — Frequency</strong><span>How often you exercise (e.g., 5 days/week)</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>I — Intensity</strong><span>How hard you work (moderate vs. vigorous)</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>T — Time</strong><span>How long each session lasts (e.g., 30–60 min)</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>T — Type</strong><span>What kind of activity (running, swimming, lifting)</span></div>
              </div>
              <p>Applying FITT helps you progressively overload muscles — gradually increasing challenge so the body adapts and improves without injury.</p>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> How would you use the FITT principle to improve cardiorespiratory endurance?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-3" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 4 — Preventing Injuries &amp; Staying Safe</h3>
              <p>Exercise-related injuries are common but largely preventable. Smart training habits protect your body for lifelong activity.</p>
              <ul class="reader-checklist">
                <li>Always warm up (5–10 min light movement) and cool down (stretching)</li>
                <li>Use proper form and technique — most injuries come from poor mechanics</li>
                <li>Wear appropriate gear and footwear</li>
                <li>Stay hydrated — drink water before, during, and after activity</li>
                <li>Rest and recover — overtraining leads to fatigue and injury</li>
                <li>Listen to your body — pain is a signal, not a badge of honor</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <strong>RICE for Minor Injuries</strong><br>
                Rest · Ice · Compression · Elevation — the first steps for sprains, strains, and minor injuries before seeking medical care.
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> What does RICE stand for and when would you use it?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-4" class="reader-section vocab-section"><h3>Chapter Vocabulary</h3>
              <div class="vocab-table">
                <div><strong>Physical activity</strong><span>Any movement produced by skeletal muscles that requires energy expenditure.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Physical fitness</strong><span>The ability to carry out daily tasks with vigor and without undue fatigue.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Cardiorespiratory endurance</strong><span>The ability of the heart and lungs to supply oxygen to working muscles during sustained activity.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Muscular strength</strong><span>The maximum force a muscle or muscle group can exert in a single effort.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Muscular endurance</strong><span>The ability of a muscle to perform repeated contractions over time.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Flexibility</strong><span>The ability of joints to move through their full range of motion.</span></div>
                <div><strong>FITT principle</strong><span>A framework for fitness programming: Frequency, Intensity, Time, and Type.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Progressive overload</strong><span>Gradually increasing the stress placed on the body during exercise to continue improving.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Warm-up</strong><span>Light activity before exercise that prepares the body and reduces injury risk.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Cool-down</strong><span>Gradual reduction in activity intensity after exercise to safely lower heart rate and stretch muscles.</span></div>
