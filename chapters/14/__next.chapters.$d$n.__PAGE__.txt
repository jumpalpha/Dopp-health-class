23:T19c2,<section id="part-0" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 1 — The Skeletal System</h3>
              <p>The skeletal system is the body's framework — 206 bones that support structure, protect organs, enable movement, produce blood cells, and store minerals like calcium and phosphorus.</p>
              <div class="reading-grid">
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Axial Skeleton</strong><span>Skull, vertebral column, rib cage — protects brain, spinal cord, and vital organs</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Appendicular Skeleton</strong><span>Arms, legs, shoulder and hip girdles — enables locomotion</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Joints</strong><span>Where bones meet; types: ball-and-socket, hinge, pivot, gliding</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Bone Health</strong><span>Calcium + Vitamin D + weight-bearing exercise = strong bones throughout life</span></div>
              </div>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <strong>Bone Growth</strong><br>
                Bones grow at growth plates (epiphyseal plates) through childhood and adolescence. Peak bone mass is reached around age 30 — building strong bones now protects against osteoporosis later.
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> What two nutrients are most important for bone health, and how do you get them?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-1" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 2 — The Muscular System</h3>
              <p>The body has over 600 muscles of three types. Muscles work by contracting — shortening to pull bones or move substances through organs.</p>
              <ul class="reader-list">
                <li><strong>Skeletal muscle</strong> — voluntary; attached to bones; enables movement; striped appearance</li>
                <li><strong>Smooth muscle</strong> — involuntary; lines organs like stomach and blood vessels</li>
                <li><strong>Cardiac muscle</strong> — involuntary; found only in the heart; never fatigues</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <strong>Muscle Pairs</strong><br>
                Muscles work in pairs — when one contracts (agonist), the opposing muscle relaxes (antagonist). Example: bicep contracts/tricep relaxes to flex the arm.
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> How do the bicep and tricep work together to move the forearm?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-2" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 3 — The Nervous System</h3>
              <p>The nervous system is the body's communication network, controlling thought, movement, sensation, and automatic functions. It has two main divisions:</p>
              <div class="reading-grid">
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Central Nervous System</strong><span>Brain + spinal cord; processes information and sends commands</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Peripheral Nervous System</strong><span>All other nerves; relays signals between CNS and body</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Somatic</strong><span>Voluntary control of skeletal muscles</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Autonomic</strong><span>Involuntary control of heart, digestion, breathing (sympathetic/parasympathetic)</span></div>
              </div>
              <p>Neurons are the basic units of the nervous system. They transmit electrical signals across synapses using chemical messengers called neurotransmitters.</p>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> What is the difference between the central and peripheral nervous system?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-3" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 4 — Caring for Your Body Systems</h3>
              <p>Lifestyle choices profoundly affect the health of skeletal, muscular, and nervous systems across a lifetime.</p>
              <ul class="reader-checklist">
                <li>Weight-bearing exercise strengthens bones and builds muscle mass</li>
                <li>Adequate calcium (1,300 mg/day for teens) and Vitamin D protect bones</li>
                <li>Helmets and protective gear prevent traumatic brain injury (TBI)</li>
                <li>Avoid tobacco — nicotine impairs nerve transmission and muscle recovery</li>
                <li>Quality sleep allows the brain to consolidate memories and repair itself</li>
                <li>Stretching maintains muscle flexibility and joint range of motion</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> How does wearing a helmet protect the nervous system?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-4" class="reader-section vocab-section"><h3>Chapter Vocabulary</h3>
              <div class="vocab-table">
                <div><strong>Skeletal system</strong><span>The framework of 206 bones that supports the body, protects organs, and enables movement.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Cartilage</strong><span>Flexible connective tissue at joints that cushions bones and prevents friction.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Tendon</strong><span>Tough connective tissue that attaches muscle to bone.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Ligament</strong><span>Connective tissue that connects bone to bone at joints.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Skeletal muscle</strong><span>Voluntary muscle attached to bones that enables body movement.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Cardiac muscle</strong><span>Involuntary muscle found only in the heart that contracts continuously without fatigue.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Neuron</strong><span>A nerve cell that transmits electrical signals throughout the nervous system.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Synapse</strong><span>The gap between neurons where chemical neurotransmitters pass signals.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Reflex</strong><span>An automatic, involuntary response to a stimulus, processed in the spinal cord.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Osteoporosis</strong><span>A disease in which bones become weak and brittle due to loss of calcium and bone density.</span></div>
              </div>
