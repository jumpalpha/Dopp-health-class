23:T18fb,<section id="part-0" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 1 — The Endocrine System</h3>
              <p>The endocrine system is a network of glands that produce hormones — chemical messengers that regulate growth, metabolism, mood, reproduction, and more.</p>
              <div class="reading-grid">
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Pituitary Gland</strong><span>The "master gland" — regulates other glands; controls growth hormone</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Thyroid</strong><span>Controls metabolism, energy, and body temperature</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Adrenal Glands</strong><span>Produce adrenaline (epinephrine) for fight-or-flight response; also cortisol</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Pancreas</strong><span>Produces insulin and glucagon to regulate blood sugar</span></div>
              </div>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <strong>Hormones &amp; Puberty</strong><br>
                During puberty, the pituitary gland signals the gonads (ovaries/testes) to produce sex hormones — estrogen, progesterone, and testosterone — triggering physical and emotional changes.
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> What is the role of the pituitary gland in the endocrine system?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-1" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 2 — Puberty &amp; Physical Development</h3>
              <p>Puberty is the process of physical and sexual maturation driven by hormonal changes. It typically begins between ages 8–13 for females and 9–14 for males.</p>
              <ul class="reader-list">
                <li><strong>Female changes:</strong> breast development, pubic/underarm hair, menstruation, hips widening</li>
                <li><strong>Male changes:</strong> testicular growth, pubic/facial hair, voice deepening, muscle development</li>
                <li><strong>Both:</strong> growth spurts, acne, body odor, emotional changes</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <strong>Normal Variation</strong><br>
                The timing of puberty varies widely and is largely genetic. Early or late development is usually normal — but persistent concerns should be discussed with a healthcare provider.
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> What hormone primarily drives puberty in males? In females?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-2" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 3 — Reproductive Systems</h3>
              <p>The male and female reproductive systems have distinct structures and functions, all regulated by the endocrine system.</p>
              <div class="reading-grid">
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Female Reproductive</strong><span>Ovaries (produce eggs/hormones), fallopian tubes, uterus, cervix, vagina</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Male Reproductive</strong><span>Testes (produce sperm/testosterone), epididymis, vas deferens, penis</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Menstrual Cycle</strong><span>~28-day cycle; prepares uterus for potential pregnancy; regulated by hormones</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Fertilization</strong><span>Sperm + egg → zygote; occurs in fallopian tube if timing aligns</span></div>
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> Where does fertilization typically occur in the female reproductive system?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-3" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 4 — Reproductive Health &amp; Care</h3>
              <p>Reproductive health involves physical, emotional, and social well-being related to the reproductive system throughout life.</p>
              <ul class="reader-checklist">
                <li>Annual gynecological exams recommended beginning in the teen years</li>
                <li>Testicular self-exams monthly — report unusual lumps to a provider</li>
                <li>Abstinence is the only 100% effective method to prevent pregnancy and STIs</li>
                <li>HPV vaccine is recommended for preteens — prevents several cancers</li>
                <li>Understanding consent is fundamental to all sexual health decisions</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> Why is the HPV vaccine recommended for teens?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-4" class="reader-section vocab-section"><h3>Chapter Vocabulary</h3>
              <div class="vocab-table">
                <div><strong>Endocrine system</strong><span>The system of glands that produce and release hormones into the bloodstream.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Hormone</strong><span>A chemical messenger produced by a gland that travels through the blood to regulate body functions.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Puberty</strong><span>The period of physical and sexual maturation driven by hormonal changes.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Estrogen</strong><span>The primary female sex hormone responsible for reproductive development and the menstrual cycle.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Testosterone</strong><span>The primary male sex hormone responsible for male reproductive development and muscle growth.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Menstruation</strong><span>The monthly shedding of the uterine lining when pregnancy does not occur.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Ovulation</strong><span>The release of a mature egg from the ovary, typically mid-cycle.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Fertilization</strong><span>The union of a sperm cell and an egg cell to form a zygote.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Insulin</strong><span>A hormone produced by the pancreas that regulates blood sugar levels.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Adrenal glands</strong><span>Glands above the kidneys that produce adrenaline and cortisol for stress response.</span></div>
              </div>
