25:T1b0e,<section id="part-0" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 1 — Foundations of Healthy Relationships</h3>
              <h4>What Makes a Relationship Healthy?</h4>
              <p>Healthy relationships — whether with friends, family, or romantic partners — share the same core qualities: mutual respect, trust, honesty, support, and open communication. When these qualities are present, relationships add to your life. When they are absent, relationships drain and damage.</p>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <div class="callout-label">Key Idea</div>
                <div>You teach people how to treat you. The boundaries you set and enforce communicate what you will and will not accept in your relationships.</div>
              </div>
              <h4>Types of Relationships</h4>
              <div class="reading-grid compact">
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Friendships</h3><p>Voluntary bonds built on shared interests, trust, and mutual support. Quality over quantity matters.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Family</h3><p>Primary relationships that shape your values, communication style, and sense of self from birth.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Romantic</h3><p>Involve deeper emotional and sometimes physical intimacy. Require the same respect and communication as all healthy relationships.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Professional/Peer</h3><p>Classmates, teammates, coworkers. Boundaries are often clearer; respect and cooperation are essential.</p></div>
              </div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-1" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 2 — Communication Skills</h3>
              <p>Communication is the foundation of every relationship. Most relationship problems are, at their core, communication problems. Learning to communicate clearly and respectfully is one of the highest-value life skills you can develop.</p>
              <h4>Components of Effective Communication</h4>
              <ul class="reader-list">
                <li><strong>"I" statements:</strong> Express how you feel without blaming. "I feel hurt when..." instead of "You always..."</li>
                <li><strong>Active listening:</strong> Full attention, no interrupting, reflecting back what you heard.</li>
                <li><strong>Nonverbal communication:</strong> Eye contact, posture, tone of voice — these carry more weight than words.</li>
                <li><strong>Assertiveness:</strong> Stating your needs clearly and respectfully, without aggression or passivity.</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-check">
                <strong>Communication Styles</strong>
                <span>Passive communicators avoid conflict but build resentment. Aggressive communicators win arguments but damage trust. Assertive communicators express needs directly while respecting others — this is the goal.</span>
              </div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-2" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 3 — Peer Pressure and Refusal Skills</h3>
              <p>Peer pressure does not always look like someone threatening you. More often it is subtle: wanting to fit in, fear of rejection, or not wanting to be the only one who says no. Building strong refusal skills protects your health and your values.</p>
              <h4>Effective Refusal Strategies</h4>
              <ul class="reader-list">
                <li>Say no clearly and without apology — "No, I'm not doing that."</li>
                <li>Suggest an alternative — "I'm not doing that, but I'll do this instead."</li>
                <li>Use the broken record technique — keep repeating no calmly.</li>
                <li>Leave the situation — you always have the right to walk away.</li>
                <li>Use delay tactics — "I need to think about it" buys time.</li>
              </ul>
            </section>
            <section id="part-3" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 4 — Unhealthy Relationships and Boundaries</h3>
              <p>Not all relationships are healthy. Recognizing the signs of unhealthy and abusive relationships early — before they escalate — is critical for your safety and well-being.</p>
              <h4>Signs of an Unhealthy Relationship</h4>
              <ul class="reader-list">
                <li>One person consistently controls or manipulates the other</li>
                <li>Disrespect, criticism, or humiliation — especially in public</li>
                <li>Jealousy framed as love or caring</li>
                <li>Pressure to change who you are or give up your other relationships</li>
                <li>Fear of your partner's or friend's reactions</li>
              </ul>
              <ul class="reader-checklist">
                <li>Everyone deserves a relationship free from fear, control, and disrespect</li>
                <li>Setting and enforcing boundaries is not selfish — it is necessary</li>
                <li>If you feel unsafe, tell a trusted adult or call the National Domestic Violence Hotline: 1-800-799-7233</li>
              </ul>
            </section>
