1f:T26a7,<section id="part-0" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 1 — Your Total Health</h3>
              <h4>What Is Health?</h4>
              <p>Health is not just about your body. It is a combination of many different areas of well-being that all work together. Think of it less like a triangle and more like a web. When one area is struggling, the others feel it too.</p>

              <div class="reading-callout">
                <div class="callout-label">Key Idea</div>
                <div>When these areas are in balance, you experience true wellness: functioning at your best. Neglect one area too long and it starts pulling the others down.</div>
              </div>

              <h4>The Many Dimensions of Health</h4>
              <div class="reading-grid compact">
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Physical</h3><p>How well your body functions day to day. Sleep 8-10 hours, eat well, move at least 60 minutes a day, and skip tobacco and drugs.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Mental</h3><p>How you think and process the world, including your ability to learn, decide, and handle challenges.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Emotional</h3><p>How you recognize, express, and manage feelings, including bouncing back from setbacks.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Social</h3><p>How you connect with others through supportive relationships built on respect and trust.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Spiritual</h3><p>A deep sense of meaning, purpose, and values: feeling that your life matters.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Financial</h3><p>Managing money in a way that reduces stress, one of the hidden drivers of poor health.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Environmental</h3><p>The health of the spaces around you, including clean air, safe neighborhoods, and access to green spaces.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Cultural</h3><p>Connection to your cultural identity, traditions, and community.</p></div>
              </div>

              <h4>The Health Continuum</h4>
              <p>Think of your health like a sliding scale, from premature death on one end to feeling your absolute best on the other. Every choice moves you one direction. Many Americans drift lower because of chronic diseases such as heart disease, obesity, and cancer. Many of these conditions are preventable with better daily habits.</p>
            </section>

            <section id="part-1" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 2 — What Affects Your Health?</h3>
              <p>Lots of things shape your health that you may not even think about. You cannot control everything, but understanding these influences helps you make smarter choices.</p>
              <ul class="reader-list">
                <li><strong>Heredity:</strong> Traits passed biologically from your parents. You cannot change your genes, but you can change how you respond to them.</li>
                <li><strong>Environment:</strong> Your surroundings, from air quality to access to doctors. Family, friends, and peers influence your choices too.</li>
                <li><strong>Culture:</strong> Beliefs, customs, and behaviors of your group shape everything from food to spiritual practices.</li>
                <li><strong>Attitude:</strong> Optimists tend to actually be healthier. A positive mindset is a real health move.</li>
                <li><strong>Media and Technology:</strong> Media constantly pushes ideas about health and lifestyle, but not all of it is accurate. Stick to .gov or .edu sites and trusted organizations like the CDC.</li>
              </ul>
            </section>

            <section id="part-2" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 3 — Health Risks and Your Behavior</h3>
              <h4>The CDC's 6 Top Risk Behaviors for People Under 24</h4>
              <ol class="reader-list">
                <li>Tobacco use</li>
                <li>Poor eating habits</li>
                <li>Not enough physical activity</li>
                <li>Alcohol and drug use</li>
                <li>Risky sexual behavior</li>
                <li>Violence and unintentional injuries</li>
              </ol>
              <p>Risk behaviors can threaten your health or the health of others. They build through cumulative risks. The more risks you stack, the worse the consequences. Speeding is risky. Texting while speeding is way more dangerous.</p>

              <h4>How to Protect Yourself</h4>
              <div class="reading-grid">
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Prevention</h3><p>Regular checkups, wearing a seatbelt, and using sunscreen are steps that keep health problems from happening or getting worse.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Abstinence</h3><p>A deliberate decision to avoid high-risk behaviors including tobacco, alcohol, drugs, and sexual activity that could harm you.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Lifestyle Factors</h3><p>Everyday personal habits such as sleep, nutrition, physical activity, and avoiding substances all shape long-term health.</p></div>
              </div>
            </section>

            <section id="part-3" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 4 — Promoting Health and Wellness</h3>
              <p>The United States spends more per person on health care than any other country, and a huge chunk of that cost could be avoided if more people had solid health education: accurate information and the skills to act on it.</p>

              <h4>Healthy People 2030</h4>
              <p>Healthy People 2030 sets national health goals every decade. Key priorities include helping all Americans live healthier, longer lives and closing health disparities. Health disparities are gaps in health outcomes across groups based on race, gender, income, location, or disability. Everyone deserves a fair shot at good health.</p>

              <h4>Health Literacy</h4>
              <p>Health literacy is your ability to find, understand, and use health information. It matters more than age, income, or education level when it comes to your overall health.</p>
              <ul class="reader-checklist">
                <li>Thinks critically before believing health claims</li>
                <li>Knows how to find reliable sources</li>
                <li>Makes decisions good for themselves and their community</li>
                <li>Can communicate health information clearly to others</li>
              </ul>

              <div class="reading-check">
                <strong>Course Goal</strong>
                <span>This course helps you become someone who takes charge of your own health with knowledge, confidence, and good judgment.</span>
              </div>
            </section>
