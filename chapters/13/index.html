Lesson 1 — Skin, Hair & Nail Care

Your skin is the body's largest organ — a protective barrier against bacteria, UV radiation, and injury. Good hygiene preserves this barrier and prevents infection.

Daily WashingUse mild soap and warm water; face wash removes excess oil and prevents acne
SunscreenSPF 30+ daily protects against UV damage and skin cancer; reapply every 2 hours outdoors
Hair CareRegular washing removes dirt and oils; brush gently to prevent breakage
Nail CareTrim nails regularly; keep clean to prevent bacterial/fungal infections
Acne Facts
Acne is caused by excess oil, dead skin cells, and bacteria clogging pores — not dirt. Hormones during puberty increase oil production. Gentle cleansing and non-comedogenic products help manage it.
Quick Check: Why is daily sunscreen use important even on cloudy days?

Lesson 2 — Dental & Eye Care

Oral health affects your whole body — gum disease is linked to heart disease and diabetes. Eyes are equally important: regular exams catch problems early.

  • Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste for 2 minutes
  • Floss once daily to remove plaque between teeth
  • Dentist visits every 6 months for cleaning and exam
  • Eye exams every 1–2 years; sooner if vision changes
  • Screen time breaks — 20-20-20 rule: every 20 min, look 20 feet away for 20 seconds
Quick Check: What is the 20-20-20 rule, and why is it helpful?

Lesson 3 — Healthcare & Checkups

Preventive care catches health problems before they become serious. Regular checkups, screenings, and vaccinations are cornerstones of good health maintenance.

Annual Physical Exam
A yearly checkup with your healthcare provider includes height/weight, blood pressure, vision/hearing screening, and immunization review. It's your chance to ask questions about your health.
  • Keep an updated health record: vaccinations, allergies, medications
  • Know your family health history — many conditions are hereditary
  • Be honest with your provider — confidentiality laws protect teens
  • Follow through on referrals to specialists
Quick Check: Why is preventive care important, even when you feel healthy?

Lesson 4 — Consumer Health & Health Products

Being a smart health consumer means critically evaluating products, services, and information before making decisions. Misinformation is widespread — especially online.

Red Flags"Miracle cures," before/after photos, testimonials, no scientific evidence
Trusted SourcesCDC, WHO, Mayo Clinic, your healthcare provider
OTC MedicationsFollow label instructions; don't combine without checking interactions
Health InsuranceCovers medical costs; understand your plan's copays, deductibles, and network
Quick Check: How can you tell if a health product or claim is trustworthy?

Chapter Vocabulary

HygienePractices that maintain cleanliness and prevent disease.
DermatologistA doctor who specializes in skin, hair, and nail conditions.
PlaqueA sticky film of bacteria that forms on teeth and can cause cavities and gum disease.
FluorideA mineral that strengthens tooth enamel and helps prevent decay.
Preventive careHealth services aimed at preventing disease before it occurs, including checkups and vaccinations.
ImmunizationProtection from disease through vaccines that stimulate the immune system.
Consumer healthThe evaluation of products, services, and information related to health.
OTC medicationOver-the-counter drugs available without a prescription.
SPFSun Protection Factor — a measure of how well sunscreen blocks UV radiation.
AcneA skin condition caused by clogged pores resulting in pimples, blackheads, and whiteheads.
