24:T17fe,<section id="part-0" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 1 — What Are Medicines?</h3>
              <p>Medicines are chemical substances used to treat, prevent, or diagnose illness. They are carefully tested for safety and effectiveness before being approved.</p>
              <div class="reading-grid">
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Prescription (Rx)</strong><span>Requires a doctor's order; used for serious/complex conditions; regulated dosing</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Over-the-Counter (OTC)</strong><span>Available without prescription; safe when used as directed on label</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Vaccines</strong><span>Stimulate immune response to provide immunity without causing disease</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Side Effects</strong><span>Unintended effects of a medicine; listed on labels; some require medical attention</span></div>
              </div>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <strong>Safe Medicine Use</strong><br>
                Always follow dosing instructions, check expiration dates, and never share prescription medications. Drug interactions can be dangerous — tell your provider all the medications you take.
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> What is the difference between prescription and OTC medications?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-1" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 2 — Drug Misuse &amp; Abuse</h3>
              <p>Drug misuse means using medication in a way other than intended — wrong dose, wrong person, or wrong purpose. Drug abuse is intentional use to alter mood or perception.</p>
              <ul class="reader-list">
                <li><strong>Tolerance</strong> — needing more of a drug to get the same effect</li>
                <li><strong>Dependence</strong> — body adapts and requires drug to function normally</li>
                <li><strong>Addiction</strong> — compulsive use despite harmful consequences; a chronic brain disorder</li>
                <li><strong>Withdrawal</strong> — physical/psychological symptoms when stopping a substance</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> What is the difference between drug tolerance and drug dependence?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-2" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 3 — Types of Drugs &amp; Their Effects</h3>
              <p>Psychoactive drugs alter brain chemistry and behavior. They are categorized by how they affect the central nervous system:</p>
              <div class="reading-grid">
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Stimulants</strong><span>Speed up CNS; increase alertness (caffeine, amphetamines, cocaine)</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Depressants</strong><span>Slow CNS; reduce anxiety (alcohol, benzodiazepines, barbiturates)</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Opioids</strong><span>Block pain receptors; high addiction potential (morphine, oxycodone, heroin)</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Hallucinogens</strong><span>Distort perception of reality (LSD, psilocybin, PCP)</span></div>
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> How do stimulants affect the central nervous system differently than depressants?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-3" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 4 — Prevention &amp; Getting Help</h3>
              <p>Understanding risk factors, building refusal skills, and knowing where to get help are essential life skills for navigating a world where drugs are present.</p>
              <ul class="reader-checklist">
                <li>Risk factors: peer pressure, stress, family history of addiction, trauma</li>
                <li>Protective factors: strong relationships, school engagement, healthy coping skills</li>
                <li>Practice refusal scripts: "No thanks, it's not for me" — short, confident, no explanation needed</li>
                <li>SAMHSA National Helpline: 1-800-662-4357 (free, confidential, 24/7)</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> Name two risk factors and two protective factors for drug misuse.</div>
            </section>
