2e:T1a7a,<section id="part-0" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 1 — Understanding STIs</h3>
              <p>Sexually transmitted infections (STIs) are infections passed from person to person primarily through sexual contact. They are among the most common infectious diseases globally, with young people ages 15–24 accounting for nearly half of new U.S. cases.</p>
              <div class="reading-grid">
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Bacterial STIs</strong><span>Chlamydia, gonorrhea, syphilis — curable with antibiotics if caught early</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Viral STIs</strong><span>HIV, herpes, HPV, hepatitis B — no cure, but manageable with treatment</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Parasitic STIs</strong><span>Pubic lice, trichomoniasis — treatable</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Asymptomatic</strong><span>Many STIs have no symptoms — infected people can spread them unknowingly</span></div>
              </div>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <strong>Testing Matters</strong><br>
                Most STIs are asymptomatic. The only way to know your status is to get tested. The CDC recommends annual STI screening for sexually active individuals under 25.
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> Why can someone have an STI and not know it?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-1" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 2 — HIV and AIDS</h3>
              <p>HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) attacks CD4+ T-cells — the commanders of the immune system. Without treatment, HIV progresses to AIDS (Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome), when the immune system can no longer fight infections.</p>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <strong>Modern HIV Treatment</strong><br>
                Antiretroviral therapy (ART) suppresses HIV to undetectable levels. People with undetectable HIV cannot transmit the virus sexually (U=U: Undetectable = Untransmittable). HIV is now a manageable chronic condition, not a death sentence.
              </div>
              <ul class="reader-list">
                <li>HIV is transmitted through blood, breast milk, semen, and vaginal fluids</li>
                <li>HIV is NOT spread through casual contact: hugging, sharing food, toilet seats</li>
                <li>PrEP (pre-exposure prophylaxis) is a daily medication that prevents HIV</li>
                <li>Needle exchange programs reduce HIV transmission among drug users</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> What does U=U mean in the context of HIV treatment?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-2" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 3 — Prevention Strategies</h3>
              <p>Prevention is the most effective approach to STIs. A combination of behavioral and biomedical strategies provides the strongest protection.</p>
              <div class="reading-grid">
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Abstinence</strong><span>100% effective when practiced consistently — the only method that completely eliminates risk</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Condoms</strong><span>85–95% effective against STIs when used correctly and consistently</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Vaccination</strong><span>HPV and hepatitis B vaccines prevent specific viral STIs</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Regular Testing</strong><span>Knowing your status enables timely treatment and prevents unknowing transmission</span></div>
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> Name three prevention strategies for STIs and explain how each works.</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-3" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 4 — Reducing Stigma &amp; Getting Help</h3>
              <p>STI stigma prevents people from getting tested, treated, and disclosing their status to partners — making the public health problem worse. Accurate information is the antidote to stigma.</p>
              <ul class="reader-checklist">
                <li>STIs are medical conditions, not moral judgments — anyone who is sexually active can get one</li>
                <li>Early treatment prevents complications (infertility, cancer, organ damage)</li>
                <li>Partner notification is an ethical responsibility — many health departments offer anonymous notification</li>
                <li>Planned Parenthood and local health clinics offer confidential, low-cost testing</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> How does stigma make the STI epidemic worse?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-4" class="reader-section vocab-section"><h3>Chapter Vocabulary</h3>
              <div class="vocab-table">
                <div><strong>STI</strong><span>Sexually Transmitted Infection — an infection passed primarily through sexual contact.</span></div>
                <div><strong>HIV</strong><span>Human Immunodeficiency Virus — the virus that attacks the immune system and can lead to AIDS.</span></div>
                <div><strong>AIDS</strong><span>Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome — the late stage of HIV when the immune system is severely compromised.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Antiretroviral therapy</strong><span>Medication that suppresses HIV to undetectable levels, preventing disease progression and transmission.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Chlamydia</strong><span>The most common bacterial STI; often asymptomatic; curable with antibiotics.</span></div>
                <div><strong>HPV</strong><span>Human Papillomavirus — the most common STI; some strains cause genital warts or cancer; preventable by vaccine.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Abstinence</strong><span>Refraining from sexual activity; the only 100% effective method to prevent STIs and pregnancy.</span></div>
                <div><strong>PrEP</strong><span>Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis — a daily medication that prevents HIV in high-risk individuals.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Asymptomatic</strong><span>Having an infection without noticeable symptoms.</span></div>
                <div><strong>U=U</strong><span>Undetectable = Untransmittable — a person on effective ART with undetectable HIV cannot sexually transmit the virus.</span></div>
              </div>
