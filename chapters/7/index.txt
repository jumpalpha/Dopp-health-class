2d:T1d24,<section id="part-0" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 1 — Healthy Family Relationships</h3>
  <h4>Types of Families</h4>
  <p>American families come in many forms. About 65% of children live with two married parents, 22% with their mother only, 5% with their father only, and 4% with neither parent. What makes a family isn't a legal definition. It's love, commitment and the bonds that form over a lifetime.</p>
  <div class="reading-grid compact">
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Nuclear</h3><p>Two parents and one or more children living together.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Single-parent</h3><p>One parent raising one or more children, often after divorce, separation or death.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Blended</h3><p>Formed when a single parent remarries, bringing children from earlier relationships together.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Extended</h3><p>Includes grandparents, aunts, uncles or cousins, sometimes living in the same home.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Adoptive &amp; Foster</h3><p>Adoption is a permanent legal family. Foster care is a temporary placement with adults who aren't relatives.</p></div>
  </div>
  <h4>Your Family and Your Health</h4>
  <ul class="reader-list">
    <li><strong>Physical health:</strong> Families provide food, clothing, shelter and medical care, set safety rules and model healthy habits.</li>
    <li><strong>Mental/emotional health:</strong> A healthy family gives you a safe place to share feelings, a sense of belonging, and <em>affirmation</em>, positive feedback that helps you feel valued.</li>
    <li><strong>Social health:</strong> Your first lessons in communicating, sharing, cooperating and settling disagreements came from your family.</li>
  </ul>
</section>
<section id="part-1" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 2 — Strengthening Family Relationships</h3>
  <h4>Traits of Strong Families</h4>
  <ul class="reader-list">
    <li><strong>Good communication:</strong> They share thoughts and feelings honestly, listen and show empathy.</li>
    <li><strong>Caring and support:</strong> They show appreciation and help each other through hard times.</li>
    <li><strong>Respect:</strong> They honor each other's opinions, privacy and individuality.</li>
    <li><strong>Commitment:</strong> They make time for each other and work on problems together.</li>
    <li><strong>Trust:</strong> Parents keep promises, and children show they are reliable and honest.</li>
  </ul>
  <h4>Coping with Family Change</h4>
  <div class="reading-grid compact">
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>New baby or adoption</h3><p>Exciting, but everyone's roles shift. Tension eases as the family adjusts.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Separation &amp; divorce</h3><p>Painful for everyone. <em>Custody</em> may go to one parent or be shared. The divorce is never the children's fault.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Remarriage</h3><p>Blended families can take 4–7 years to feel natural. Patience and respect help.</p></div>
    <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Death, moving, money</h3><p>Grief, a new school or a lost job all add stress. Talking openly helps the whole family cope.</p></div>
  </div>
  <div class="reading-callout"><div class="callout-label">Key Idea</div><div>The most important coping strategy is honest, open communication. Talking about your feelings, even when it's uncomfortable, lowers stress and helps family members support each other.</div></div>
</section>
<section id="part-2" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 3 — Help for Families</h3>
  <h4>Violence in Families</h4>
  <p><em>Abuse</em> is the physical, mental, emotional or sexual mistreatment of one person by another. When it happens between family members, it is called <em>domestic violence</em>. It can be spousal abuse, child abuse (including <em>neglect</em>) or elder abuse. Abuse is always wrong, and it is a crime.</p>
  <h4>Recognize · Resist · Report</h4>
  <ul class="reader-checklist">
    <li><strong>Recognize:</strong> Abuse is a crime. Take any report seriously.</li>
    <li><strong>Resist:</strong> If it's safe, get away and reach a trusted adult.</li>
    <li><strong>Report:</strong> Tell someone who can help: a trusted adult, school counselor, doctor, hotline or police.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>The <em>cycle of violence</em>, abuse repeating from one generation to the next, can be broken with professional help. Asking for help takes courage. Crisis centers, family counseling and support groups all help families heal.</p>
  <div class="reading-callout"><div class="callout-label">Get Help</div><div>National Domestic Violence Hotline: <strong>1-800-799-7233</strong> (or text START to 88788) · Childhelp Child Abuse Hotline: <strong>1-800-422-4453</strong> · 988 Suicide &amp; Crisis Lifeline. All are free and open 24/7.</div></div>
</section>
