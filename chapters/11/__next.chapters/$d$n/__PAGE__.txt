25:T2057,<section id="part-0" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 1 — Weight, Health &amp; Body Image</h3>
              <p>Healthy weight is about more than a number on the scale. <strong>Body composition</strong> — the ratio of fat to lean tissue — is a more meaningful measure of health. Factors like genetics, metabolism, age, and activity level all influence where your body settles.</p>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <strong>Body Image vs. Body Reality</strong><br>
                Body image is how you <em>see</em> yourself, which often differs from reality. Media, social comparison, and peer pressure can distort body image and lead to unhealthy behaviors.
              </div>
              <div class="reading-grid">
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Underweight</strong><span>BMI below 18.5; may signal nutritional deficiency or eating disorder</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Healthy Weight</strong><span>BMI 18.5–24.9; associated with lower disease risk</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Overweight</strong><span>BMI 25–29.9; increased risk for chronic conditions</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Obese</strong><span>BMI 30+; significantly elevated health risk</span></div>
              </div>
              <p>BMI is a useful screening tool but doesn't directly measure body fat — athletes may have high BMI with low fat. Healthcare providers use BMI alongside other assessments for a complete picture.</p>
              <div class="reading-check">
                <strong>Quick Check:</strong> Why isn't BMI always an accurate measure of an individual's health?
              </div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-1" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 2 — Achieving &amp; Maintaining a Healthy Weight</h3>
              <p>Weight management is about balance — <strong>energy in</strong> (calories consumed) vs. <strong>energy out</strong> (calories burned). No single diet or trick achieves lasting results; sustainable habits make the difference.</p>
              <ul class="reader-list">
                <li><strong>Set realistic goals</strong> — aim for 0.5–1 lb per week, not crash losses</li>
                <li><strong>Eat mindfully</strong> — recognize hunger cues, avoid emotional eating</li>
                <li><strong>Move consistently</strong> — 60 min daily activity for teens (CDC)</li>
                <li><strong>Sleep enough</strong> — poor sleep raises hunger hormones and fat storage</li>
                <li><strong>Limit ultra-processed foods</strong> — high in calories, low in nutrients</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <strong>Fad Diets Warning</strong><br>
                Extreme or fad diets promise fast results but often cause muscle loss, nutritional deficiencies, and weight regain. Evidence-based approaches focus on gradual change and overall lifestyle.
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check">
                <strong>Quick Check:</strong> What does "energy balance" mean, and why does it matter for weight management?
              </div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-2" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 3 — Eating Disorders</h3>
              <p>Eating disorders are serious mental health conditions involving disordered eating behaviors that harm physical and emotional health. They affect all genders, ages, and body types.</p>
              <div class="reading-grid">
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Anorexia Nervosa</strong><span>Extreme restriction of food; distorted body image; fear of weight gain</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Bulimia Nervosa</strong><span>Cycles of bingeing then purging; often done in secret</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Binge Eating Disorder</strong><span>Frequent episodes of eating large amounts without purging; most common eating disorder</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Orthorexia</strong><span>Obsessive focus on "clean" or "pure" eating to the point of harm</span></div>
              </div>
              <p>Warning signs include dramatic weight changes, obsession with food/body, avoiding meals, or mood changes around eating. Early intervention dramatically improves outcomes.</p>
              <ul class="reader-checklist">
                <li>Treatment involves medical, nutritional, and psychological support</li>
                <li>Recovery is possible — most people improve with professional care</li>
                <li>Avoid diet talk and body shaming around peers — it can trigger disorders</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-check">
                <strong>Quick Check:</strong> Name two warning signs of an eating disorder a concerned friend might notice.
              </div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-3" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 4 — Building a Positive Relationship with Food</h3>
              <p>A healthy relationship with food means eating for nourishment and enjoyment — not guilt or fear. <strong>Intuitive eating</strong> encourages tuning into hunger and fullness cues rather than rigid rules.</p>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <strong>All Foods Fit Principle</strong><br>
                Labeling foods as "good" or "bad" creates guilt and shame. Balance, variety, and moderation allow for occasional treats without derailing overall health.
              </div>
              <ul class="reader-list">
                <li>Eat regular meals — skipping leads to overeating later</li>
                <li>Focus on how food makes you <em>feel</em>, not just calories</li>
                <li>Cook and share meals — social eating supports mental health</li>
                <li>Challenge media messages about "perfect" bodies</li>
              </ul>
              <p>If you or someone you know struggles with eating behaviors, reach out to a trusted adult, school counselor, or the National Alliance for Eating Disorders helpline (1-866-662-1235, weekdays).</p>
              <div class="reading-check">
                <strong>Quick Check:</strong> What is intuitive eating, and how does it differ from dieting?
              </div>
            </section>
