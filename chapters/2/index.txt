2e:T2f46,<section id="part-0" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 1 — Building Health Skills</h3>
              <p><strong>Health skills</strong>, also called life skills, are the tools and strategies you use to protect, maintain, and improve your health. Once you build them, they stick with you for life.</p>

              <div class="reading-callout">
                <div class="callout-label">Key Idea</div>
                <div>Health skills are not just facts to memorize. They are actions you practice: communicating, saying no, finding reliable information, managing stress, making decisions, and setting goals.</div>
              </div>

              <h4>The Core Health Skills</h4>
              <div class="reading-grid compact">
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Communication</h3><p>Share ideas and feelings clearly while genuinely listening to others. Use "I" statements, stay calm, and listen actively.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Refusal Skills</h3><p>Say no firmly, respectfully, and confidently when pressured into something that goes against your values or health.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Conflict Resolution</h3><p>End disagreements through cooperation and problem-solving. Compromise often means both sides give a little to reach a result everyone can live with.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Accessing Information</h3><p>Find reliable health information from .gov and .edu sites, health professionals, and recently published work from respected experts.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Analyzing Influences</h3><p>Recognize what shapes your choices: values, family, culture, friends, and media. Self-awareness leads to better decisions.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Healthful Behaviors</h3><p>Make good habits part of your daily routine, including eating well, sleeping enough, staying active, and avoiding harmful substances.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Stress Management</h3><p>Stress is normal, but too much unmanaged stress can make you sick. Exercise, relaxation, and time management can help.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Advocacy</h3><p>Take action to encourage others to make healthy choices or support health-related causes.</p></div>
              </div>
            </section>

            <section id="part-1" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 2 — Making Responsible Decisions and Setting Goals</h3>
              <p>Good decisions start with your <strong>values</strong>: the beliefs and attitudes that guide the way you live. When a decision feels hard, talking it through with a trusted adult or family member can help.</p>

              <h4>The 6-Step Decision-Making Process</h4>
              <ol class="reader-list">
                <li><strong>State the situation:</strong> What exactly do I need to decide?</li>
                <li><strong>List the options:</strong> What are all the possible choices?</li>
                <li><strong>Weigh the outcomes:</strong> Use HELP: Healthful, Ethical, Legal, and Parent Approval.</li>
                <li><strong>Consider values:</strong> Does this choice align with what matters to me?</li>
                <li><strong>Make a decision and act:</strong> Commit to the healthiest responsible choice.</li>
                <li><strong>Evaluate the decision:</strong> How did it go? What would I do differently?</li>
              </ol>

              <h4>Setting Goals: The S.M.A.R.T. Framework</h4>
              <p>Goals are things you aim for that take planning and effort. The reason many goals fail is that they are too vague. S.M.A.R.T. goals make the target clear.</p>
              <div class="reading-grid compact">
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Specific</h3><p>Clearly define what you want. "Drink 8 glasses of water every day" is clearer than "be healthier."</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Measurable</h3><p>Make sure you can track progress. "Run 3 times a week" is easier to measure than "exercise more."</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Achievable</h3><p>Challenge yourself but stay realistic. Training for a 5K can be achievable; a marathon next week is probably not.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Relevant</h3><p>Connect the goal to something that matters. If you want to make the basketball team, stamina is relevant.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Time-bound</h3><p>Give yourself a deadline. "Cut out soda for 30 days" is stronger than "someday I will eat better."</p></div>
              </div>

              <div class="reading-check">
                <strong>Upgrade the Goal</strong>
                <span>Weak goal: "I want to sleep more." S.M.A.R.T. goal: "I will be in bed by 10:30 PM on school nights for the next two weeks."</span>
              </div>

              <p>Short-term goals are stepping stones to long-term goals. Build an <strong>action plan</strong>: write down your goal, list the steps, identify who can support you, set a timeline, create checkpoints, and celebrate when you succeed.</p>
            </section>

            <section id="part-2" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 3 — Being a Health-Literate Consumer</h3>
              <p>Every day you make buying decisions. When it comes to health products and services, being a smart <strong>health consumer</strong> matters. One of the biggest influences on buying decisions is <strong>advertising</strong>: media messages designed to get you to buy something.</p>

              <h4>Common Advertising Techniques to Watch For</h4>
              <div class="reading-grid compact">
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Bandwagon</h3><p>"Everyone's doing it!"</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Testimonial</h3><p>"It worked for me!"</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Rich and Famous</h3><p>Makes a product look luxurious or celebrity-approved.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Good Times</h3><p>Shows happy people using the product.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Great Outdoors</h3><p>Uses nature imagery to make something seem healthy.</p></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><h3>Free Gifts</h3><p>Makes deals feel too good to pass up.</p></div>
              </div>

              <h4>How to Shop Smart</h4>
              <ul class="reader-checklist">
                <li>Read product labels. Ingredients are listed by weight from most to least. Active ingredients are what make a product work.</li>
                <li>Use comparison shopping. Compare cost, quality, features, and safety ratings before buying.</li>
                <li>Check the warranty, which is a written promise to repair or replace a product if it does not work properly.</li>
                <li>Look for safety certifications such as UL for appliances and ANSI for helmets and protective gear.</li>
                <li>Use trusted health sources such as .gov, .edu, the CDC, or the American Medical Association.</li>
              </ul>
            </section>

            <section id="part-3" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 4 — Managing Consumer Problems</h3>
              <h4>When a Product Does Not Work</h4>
              <ol class="reader-list">
                <li>Read the warranty and return policy before you buy.</li>
                <li>Follow the instructions carefully.</li>
                <li>If it still does not work, contact the manufacturer.</li>
                <li>If the issue is unresolved, reach out to the Better Business Bureau, consumer advocates, or the FDA.</li>
              </ol>

              <h4>Watch Out for Health Fraud</h4>
              <p><strong>Health fraud</strong>, also called quackery, is the sale of worthless products or services claiming to cure or prevent health problems. Weight loss and beauty products are common targets.</p>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <div class="callout-label">Red Flags</div>
