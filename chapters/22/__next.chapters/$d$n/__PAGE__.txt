20:T1b2f,<section id="part-0" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 1 — Marijuana</h3>
              <p>Marijuana (cannabis) is the most commonly used illegal drug among teens. THC (tetrahydrocannabinol) is the psychoactive compound that creates its effects by binding to cannabinoid receptors in the brain.</p>
              <div class="reading-grid">
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Short-Term Effects</strong><span>Euphoria, impaired memory, slowed reaction time, increased appetite, anxiety</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Long-Term Effects</strong><span>Memory/learning impairment, lung damage (if smoked), risk of Cannabis Use Disorder</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Teen Brain Risk</strong><span>THC disrupts normal brain development; linked to lower IQ and mental health problems</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Vaping/Edibles</strong><span>Concentrated forms increase overdose risk; edibles have delayed effects</span></div>
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> Why is marijuana use particularly risky for teenagers compared to adults?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-1" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 2 — Stimulants &amp; Cocaine</h3>
              <p>Stimulant drugs like cocaine, methamphetamine, and Adderall (when misused) dramatically increase dopamine in the brain's reward system, creating intense pleasure — and severe crashes.</p>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <strong>Meth &amp; the Brain</strong><br>
                Methamphetamine releases 3x more dopamine than cocaine. It destroys dopamine receptors over time, leaving users unable to feel pleasure from normal activities — a condition called anhedonia.
              </div>
              <ul class="reader-list">
                <li>Cocaine: short-lived high, intense crash, high addiction potential; can cause cardiac arrest</li>
                <li>Meth: cheap, long-lasting, devastatingly addictive; causes severe dental decay and skin sores</li>
                <li>Adderall misuse: not "safer" because it's a prescription — still carries addiction and cardiac risk</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> What is anhedonia, and why does meth cause it?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-2" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 3 — Opioids &amp; Heroin</h3>
              <p>The opioid epidemic is the deadliest drug crisis in U.S. history. At its peak it killed over 80,000 Americans a year. Deaths have dropped since, but opioids still killed about 44,600 people in 2025 (CDC). Many opioid addictions start with legally prescribed painkillers.</p>
              <div class="reading-callout">
                <strong>Fentanyl Crisis</strong><br>
                Illicitly manufactured fentanyl — 50x stronger than heroin — is now found in counterfeit pills and other drugs. A dose the size of a few grains of salt can be fatal. There is no safe "recreational" opioid use.
              </div>
              <ul class="reader-checklist">
                <li>Opioids bind to brain receptors, blocking pain and creating euphoria</li>
                <li>Respiratory depression (breathing stops) is the mechanism of overdose death</li>
                <li>Naloxone (Narcan) can reverse an overdose — available without prescription in most states</li>
                <li>Medication-assisted treatment (buprenorphine, methadone) saves lives</li>
              </ul>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> Why is fentanyl especially dangerous compared to other opioids?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-3" class="reader-section"><h3>Lesson 4 — Inhalants, Club Drugs &amp; Prevention</h3>
              <p>Some of the most dangerous substances are legal products misused for their psychoactive effects. Inhalants are particularly common among younger teens due to easy access.</p>
              <div class="reading-grid">
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Inhalants</strong><span>Huffing glue, paint, aerosols; can cause sudden death (SDS) even on first use</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>MDMA (Ecstasy)</strong><span>Damages serotonin neurons; causes dehydration, overheating, cardiac risk</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Ketamine/GHB</strong><span>Dissociative drugs; used as date rape drugs; dangerous combinations with alcohol</span></div>
                <div class="reading-tile"><strong>Prevention</strong><span>Education, strong family bonds, healthy coping, refusal skills, community programs</span></div>
              </div>
              <div class="reading-check"><strong>Quick Check:</strong> What makes inhalants especially dangerous compared to other drugs?</div>
            </section>
            <section id="part-4" class="reader-section vocab-section"><h3>Chapter Vocabulary</h3>
              <div class="vocab-table">
                <div><strong>THC</strong><span>Tetrahydrocannabinol — the psychoactive compound in marijuana that produces its effects.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Dopamine</strong><span>A neurotransmitter in the brain's reward system; many addictive drugs trigger massive dopamine release.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Methamphetamine</strong><span>A highly addictive stimulant that causes intense euphoria followed by severe physical and mental damage.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Opioid</strong><span>A class of drugs — including heroin, morphine, and fentanyl — that bind to pain receptors and cause euphoria.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Fentanyl</strong><span>A synthetic opioid 50x stronger than heroin; now the leading cause of overdose deaths in the U.S.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Naloxone</strong><span>A medication that rapidly reverses opioid overdose; available as Narcan nasal spray.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Inhalants</strong><span>Volatile substances inhaled to produce psychoactive effects; can cause sudden death on first use.</span></div>
                <div><strong>MDMA</strong><span>3,4-methylenedioxymethamphetamine (Ecstasy/Molly) — a stimulant/hallucinogen that damages serotonin neurons.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Anhedonia</strong><span>The inability to feel pleasure from normally enjoyable activities, often caused by drug-induced receptor damage.</span></div>
                <div><strong>Gateway drug</strong><span>A substance (like alcohol or marijuana) whose early use is associated with higher likelihood of using harder drugs later.</span></div>
