Chapter 9: Resolving Conflicts and Preventing Violence

4 lessons · vocabulary · quick checks

Lesson 1 — Causes of Conflict

A conflict is any disagreement, struggle or fight. Because people are different, conflicts are a normal part of life. The goal is to manage them before they get out of hand. Handled well, conflicts can actually make relationships stronger.

Common Causes

Misunderstandings

Two people read the same thing differently, especially across backgrounds, cultures or ages.

Power struggles

Who gets to make the decisions or set the rules.

Jealousy & envy

Feeling left behind or replaced.

Property disputes

Arguments over borrowed or damaged belongings.

Conflicting values

Different beliefs about how people should act.

Lack of respect

Rudeness, prejudice or treating someone as less than equal.

Key Idea
The feelings most likely to make a conflict escalate (get more serious) are hurt pride, embarrassment and the desire for revenge. Noticing them in yourself is one of the best ways to prevent violence.

Lesson 2 — Resolving Conflicts

Negotiation, Step by Step

  • 1. Calm down first. Never negotiate while emotions are too hot to think clearly.
  • 2. Each side explains. Use "I" messages ("I feel… when… because…") and active listening. No interrupting.
  • 3. Ask clarifying questions so both sides really understand each other.
  • 4. Brainstorm solutions without judging them yet.
  • 5. Weigh the pros and cons together.
  • 6. Agree on a solution. Aim for win-win, or the best compromise.
  • 7. Follow up later to see if it worked.

Mediation and Peer Mediation

When negotiating isn't enough, mediation brings in a neutral third party. In peer mediation, trained students help classmates settle conflicts. The keys are neutrality, confidentiality and clear ground rules.

Important
Peer mediation is NOT for situations involving violence, weapons or crimes. Those go to school administrators or law enforcement.

Lesson 3 — Understanding Violence

Violence is the threatened or actual use of physical force to harm a person or damage property. Uncontrolled anger, the need to control others, prejudice and revenge all contribute. Alcohol and drugs make violence more likely because they lower inhibitions and impair judgment.

Firearm Safety
Guns are now the leading cause of death for U.S. children and teens. Teens who carry guns are more likely to be hurt by one. Don't carry a weapon. Guns at home should be stored unloaded and locked.

Types of Violence

Assault

An unlawful physical attack or threat of attack. About half happen between people who know each other.

School threats

Know your school's emergency plan. Report threats, even ones that sound like jokes. Speaking up saves lives.

Sexual violence

Any unwelcome sexual contact. Most victims are under 18.

Hate crimes

Crimes motivated by hatred of a group because of race, religion, disability, sexual orientation or other traits.

Bullying and Cyberbullying

Bullying is repeated, deliberate hurtful behavior where one side has more power. Cyberbullying happens through texts, social media and games, and it can follow you home. Don't respond or retaliate. Save the evidence, block the person, and tell a trusted adult. When you see it happen, be an upstander: speak up, support the target, or get help.

Lesson 4 — Preventing and Overcoming Abuse

Warning Signs of an Abusive Relationship

  • Pressures you into sexual activity
  • Pushes the relationship to be serious or exclusive very fast
  • Acts jealous or possessive, controlling where you go or who you see
  • Yells, swears or attacks you emotionally
  • Threatens physical violence
Key Idea
Controlling behavior is NOT love. These are warning signs of abuse, not signs of caring.

Forms of Abuse

Physical

Hitting, kicking, shoving, biting or throwing things.

Emotional

Verbal abuse, humiliation, threats or isolating someone from friends and family.

Sexual

Any unwanted sexual contact or pressure.

Stalking

Repeatedly following, harassing or threatening someone, in person or online.

About 1 in 9 high school girls reports sexual violence by a dating partner. Alcohol is a major factor, and some attackers use date rape drugs. Never leave your drink unattended, and don't finish a drink that smells or tastes strange.

Get Help
RAINN Sexual Assault Hotline: 1-800-656-4673 · love is respect (teen dating abuse): text LOVEIS to 22522 · 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline. Survivors deserve support, not shame. Abuse is never the victim's fault.

Chapter Vocabulary

ConflictAny disagreement, struggle, or fight.
Interpersonal conflictsConflicts between two or more people or groups.
EscalateTo become more serious or intense.
NegotiationThe use of communication and compromise to settle a disagreement.
MediationBringing in a neutral third party to help others resolve their conflicts peacefully.
ConfidentialityRespecting the privacy of both parties and keeping details of a discussion secret.
Peer mediationA process in which specially trained students help other students resolve conflicts peacefully.
ViolenceThe threatened or actual use of physical force or power to harm another person or damage property.
AssaultAn unlawful physical attack or threat of attack.
Random violenceViolence committed for no particular reason against an unrelated victim.
HomicideThe willful killing of one human being by another.
Sexual violenceAny form of unwelcome sexual contact directed at an individual.
BullyingRepeated, deliberate hurtful behavior by someone who has more power than the target.
CyberbullyingBullying that happens through phones, social media, games, or other technology.
Physical abuseA pattern of intentionally causing bodily harm or injury to another person.
Emotional abuseA pattern of attacking another person's emotional development and sense of worth.
Verbal abuseThe use of words to mistreat or injure another person; a form of emotional abuse.
StalkingRepeatedly following, harassing, or threatening an individual in person or online.
